# §4.4 Constrained Extrema — instructor lecture plan (50 min)

Companion to the book section `book/04-change/04-constrained.qmd`, Problem Set 8, and the
constrained-extrema studio. Cognition-first: nothing is introduced before the class needs it.

## Destination
Given a chemical quantity to optimize and a fixed budget (concentration, length, energy),
students can (1) name objective and constraint, (2) eliminate a variable and reuse the §4.3
method, (3) explain the optimum geometrically (tangency, parallel gradients), (4) predict how
the optimum shifts with stoichiometry or stiffness, and (5) say what the multiplier means.

## Cognitive starting point (what they own)
y' = 0 and y'' for one-variable functions (§4.3); the product and chain rules (§4.2); the dot
product and "perpendicular" (§2.3); gradient-as-vector is new but only needs partial derivatives
= ordinary derivatives with the other letter frozen.

## The driving gap
"Make [A][B] as large as possible" has no answer — until a budget [A]+[B]=C appears. Our one-variable
tool cannot see two knobs. What does the budget do to the problem?

## Reasoning spine
objective with two knobs -> unbounded without a budget -> constraint removes one degree of freedom
-> substitute -> one-variable extremum (4.3) -> geometry: constraint tangent to a contour
-> gradients parallel -> lambda -> test on unequal cases (stoichiometry, stiffness)
-> lambda is a price (tension, 1/T) -> next gap: 10^23 knobs and one energy budget.

## Board plan (left to right)
RECALL | GAP | MODEL (substitution) | GEOMETRY (contours, ∇f∥∇g) | CASES (a,b / stiffness / modes) | SIGNATURES (λ units, price) | NEXT (10²³ variables → Boltzmann)

## Predictions to extract before showing results
1. Yield curve example: does the best split depend on the objective only, or also on the budget? (both)
2. k[A][B]²: even split or not? Which reagent gets more? (not even; B twice A)
3. Heat sharing from E₁=8: which body is hotter, which way does energy flow? (body 1 hotter; flows 1→2)

## Anticipated misconceptions (and the fast repair)
- **"Set both partials to zero."** Finds the unconstrained stationary point (the origin). Repair: show the origin does not satisfy the constraint; restate the condition as ∇f = λ∇g.
- **"The optimum is always the even split."** Repair: the exponent family and the stiffness example — the budget divides in stoichiometric ratio / inversely with stiffness.
- **Stopping at the stationary point.** Repair: the 4-step rhythm; classify with the restricted second derivative, compare endpoints.
- **λ as an arbitrary symbol.** Repair: ask for its units every time (objective per budget) and tie to tension and 1/T.
- **Partial derivative confusion.** Repair: "it is the one-variable rule with the other letter treated as a number" — do ∂(AB)/∂A = B aloud.

## Worked numbers (all verified)
- Reagents: C=2.00 M → [A]=[B]=1.00 M, q=1.00 M², λ=1.00 M; dq*/dC = C/2.
- Chain E=(r₁−1)²+½(r₂−1)², L=2.6: r₁=1.20, r₂=1.40, E=0.120, tension 0.40 = λ = dE*/dL.
- Termolecular [A]²[B], 0.90 M: [A]=0.60, [B]=0.30, q=0.108 M³ (PS8 worked example).
- Heat sharing a=3, b=2, E=10: E₁=6, E₂=4, T=2, λ=1/T=0.5.

## Synthesis map
Question we could not answer (best split under a budget) → constraint as elimination and as tangency
→ prediction (stoichiometric ratio; inverse stiffness; equal temperatures) → measurement that tests
it (initial rate vs feed ratio; strain partition; heat flow) → remaining limitation (one budget, two
variables, idealized models).

## Bridge
With 10²³ molecules and a single energy budget, which energy distribution is most probable?
The same stationary condition applied to many variables gives the Boltzmann distribution (Module 3).
